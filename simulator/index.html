Open source · ESP32-S3 · 2.13″ e-paper

An e-reader you can build in an evening.

One board, about twenty dollars, no soldering. You flash it from a browser tab, join it to your Wi-Fi, and drop EPUBs onto it over the network. Then you read them on actual electronic paper, with a thumbwheel to turn pages.

What you need

One thing is mandatory. Everything else is a choice about whether it lives on your desk or in your pocket.

No soldering, no breadboard, no level shifters. The board ships assembled; the work is software.

Getting it running

  1. Flash it from this site

    Plug the board in and use the installer. It talks to the board over USB from your browser — nothing to install, no toolchain.

  2. Point it at your Wi-Fi

    First boot opens a setup hotspot. Pick your network once; the reader then prints its own address on the panel.

  3. Send it books

    Open that address, drop in an EPUB. Your browser converts it and uploads it in one step — no cable, no companion app, no account.

The catch

None of this has run on a physical board. The firmware compiles, and it's been reviewed line by line — that review found real bugs, including one that meant it never built at all. But the display driver, the pin mapping and the refresh timing are read off Elecrow's schematic and datasheets, not measured. The likeliest first result is a board that boots fine and shows nothing.

It was also built with an AI assistant (Claude), directed and reviewed by a person. That's worth knowing when you decide how much to trust a pin number.

So: the simulator is real and complete, and you can judge the whole reading experience from it today. The hardware side is a well-researched starting point that wants a multimeter. If you have one of these boards, a report either way is the most useful thing anyone could send.
