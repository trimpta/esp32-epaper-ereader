ESP32-S3 · 2.13″ e-paper · open source

A pocket e-reader you build yourself.

Rotary Reader turns a $20 Elecrow CrowPanel into a real e-ink reader: convert an EPUB in your browser, send it over Wi-Fi, and read it on actual electronic paper — with a rotary dial to turn pages and no screen to keep you awake.

The simulator is the whole device, in a browser tab — no hardware required. Install flashes a connected ESP32-S3 straight from this page over Web Serial.

What it actually is

Three pieces that fit together: firmware for the board, a browser-based EPUB converter, and a simulator that lets you use the whole interface before you own anything.

Zero layout work on-device

Line breaks and page breaks are computed once, in your browser, and baked into a compact binary book file. The ESP32 just seeks to an offset and draws — no EPUB parsing, no ZIP inflation, no re-flowing text on a microcontroller.

Real e-ink behavior

Fast partial refreshes for page turns, a periodic full refresh to clear ghosting, and a long-press to force one when it builds up. The simulator models all of it, so what you preview is what the panel does.

Three inputs, six gestures

A rotary encoder plus MENU and EXIT — each with a short and long press. MENU opens the book's own menu, EXIT opens Home, and holding EXIT sleeps the device. That's the entire control scheme.

Books over Wi-Fi

The reader hosts a small upload page on your network. Convert in the browser, upload, and it appears in the library — no SD card, because this board doesn't have a slot.

Reading stats & bookmarks

Resume position, bookmarks, pages read today, an average pace and an estimated finish date — kept in a small file on the device, written sparingly so page turns don't burn flash write cycles.

Five games, because why not

Lights Out, Minesweeper, Sudoku, Hangman and Tic-Tac-Toe (with a real minimax opponent), all driven by the same dial-and-two-buttons the reader uses.

How you'd set one up

Nothing here needs a soldering iron. The board arrives assembled; the work is flashing it once and then feeding it books.

Get the board

An Elecrow CrowPanel ESP32 2.13″ — ESP32-S3, e-paper panel, rotary dial and two buttons already on it. Optionally a small LiPo and a printed case.

Flash the firmware

Plug it in over USB-C and use the installer — it talks to the board from your browser, no toolchain to set up. Or build it yourself with PlatformIO.

Join it to Wi-Fi

First boot opens a setup hotspot. Pick your network, and the reader prints its address on the panel — no app, no account, nothing phoning home.

Send it a book

Open that address, drop in an EPUB, and it converts and uploads in one step. Turn the dial and start reading.

Where this honestly stands

🤖 This project is "vibe coded." The firmware, converter, simulator and docs were written collaboratively with Claude, directed and reviewed by a human — and without physical access to the board.

The firmware compiles clean and has been reviewed end-to-end (that review caught real bugs, including one that meant it never built at all). But pin mappings, the display driver class and refresh timing are reasoned from Elecrow's schematic and datasheets — not measured. Treat them as needing a multimeter, not as facts.

If you have one of these boards, a hardware report — working or not — is the single most useful thing you could contribute.
